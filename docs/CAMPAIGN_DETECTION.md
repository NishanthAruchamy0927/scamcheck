# Scam Campaign Detection (Phase 8)

## Overview
Campaign detection is the process of correlating isolated scam investigations into organized, large-scale structural threat actor campaigns. 

## The Engine
The `CorrelationEngine` analyzes new investigations as they are persisted. 
It does **not** rely on "black box AI" to magically link cases. Instead, it relies on verifiable, explainable graph intersections (Phase 7).

## Detection Criteria
A `ScamCampaign` (Candidate) is automatically generated if:
1. Two or more investigations share at least `N` distinct extracted entities. (e.g. they both share the exact same domain `fakejobs.com` AND the exact same phone number `+915551234`).
2. The investigations occurred within a predefined `timeWindowDays` (e.g. 14 days), representing temporal proximity.
3. The shared entities are not on the benign suppression list (e.g., `gmail.com`, `linkedin.com`), preventing Google from being flagged as a campaign.

## The Candidate Lifecycle
When a campaign is generated, it is explicitly labelled as a **CANDIDATE**. It is not a confirmed threat. 
1. `CANDIDATE`: Generated by the correlation engine.
2. `UNDER_REVIEW`: An analyst is investigating the linkage.
3. `CONFIRMED`: A human analyst with the `SECURITY_ANALYST` role has reviewed the evidence and confirmed it is a campaign.
4. `REJECTED`: The correlation was a false positive (e.g. legitimate shared infrastructure).

## Explainability
Every Campaign includes a `reason` and a `correlationScore`.
The system will output reasons like: `"Shared 2 entities: URL, PHONE"` rather than `"AI says these are related"`. This ensures analysts can trust and verify the output.
